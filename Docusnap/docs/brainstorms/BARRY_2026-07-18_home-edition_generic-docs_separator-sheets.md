# Barry the Brainstormer — Home edition · Generic document type · Separator sheets
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status:** BRAINSTORM ONLY — nothing designed, built or committed. Any item picked up from here goes through the normal gate (specialist advisors + Oracle, control-test-first, kill-switched).

**Owner's prompt:** (1) a home/family edition — a single home user or family buys the core to scan, file and manage home documents; (2) a generic document type — businesses and home users file arbitrary docs (not POs/invoices), retrieved by full-text search plus user-added identifying fields; (3) a printable page-separator sheet — barcode + printed number underneath, slotted into the scan pile as the split signal, barcode value cross-checked against the OCR'd number.

**Grounding verified in the codebase before the brainstorm:** full-text search is live (`documents.ocr_text`, 50k cap, LIKE scan, no FTS index); an auto-separation pre-pass exists (`auto_separate_enabled`, default ON, known-template first pages only; watch folder not covered); ref-less types are first-class (only Issuer + Date forced); no barcode lib ships (license gate denies LGPL/GPL; zxing-cpp Apache-2.0 is the clean candidate; OpenCV forbidden).

---

## Barry's verdict

Yes to all three — and they're better as a package than any one alone. The generic document type is the **commercial lever** (it removes the biggest objection in both home and business: "my documents aren't invoices"). The separator sheet is the **marketing hook** (it's the 30-second demo that sells the product). The home edition is the **market bet** — worth making, but as a mode plus a cheaper licence on the same binary, never a second product.

Two things to change. First, the separator cross-check: make the barcode and the printed number an *either-passes* pair, not an and-gate — explained below. Second, the home edition must soften the two forced fields (Issuer and Date), or the first hour fails on the first undated warranty card.

The good news that shrinks the build: you're closer than you think. Full-text search is already live — every document's OCR text is searchable today. Ref-less types are already first-class. And an automatic separation pre-pass already exists for known layouts — the separator sheet slots in as its first rung rather than a new subsystem. Most of this is packaging existing capability as a promise.

## Making each idea better

**Home edition — a mode, not a fork.** A first-run fork: "Home or Business?" Same installer, same engine; the licence key decides what's available. Home Mode hides Users, Audit, Templates admin, the LAN client and the token builders, relabels "Document Issuer" as **"From"**, and replaces confidence percentages with three plain badges: *Filed automatically / Please check / Couldn't read*. The essential unblocking work: "Unknown Company" becomes a friendly "Not sure — file it anyway" landing in a *Miscellaneous* area (a shoebox, not a failure), and a one-click **"No date on it — use today"** stamps the scan date, visibly marked as the filed date. That one button probably decides whether the first hour succeeds. Ship a Home preset pack (Utility Bill, Warranty & Receipt, School Letter, Vehicle, Medical, Tax) via the existing tick-to-add catalog, re-fixture the practice run with a gas bill and a school letter, and don't surface supplier graduation at all — a household gets one gas bill a month, so per-supplier trust takes a year; lean on the clean auto-file path and under-promise. Price around **£79 one-time (launch £59)** as a household licence, with a pay-the-difference upgrade to the £299 tier. The pitch is structurally true: *"Your family's filing cabinet. It never leaves your house."*

**Generic type — design it as a funnel, not a bucket.** The review card should be a glance, not a form: thumbnail, best-guess sender, best date found, an auto-picked title, a two-line snippet — Enter files it. The generic type softens the hard requirements by design (date falls back to scan date; blank sender files under "General"). Draw one clear line users can hold in their head: **if the document prints it, it's a field; if you'd type it, it's a tag.** Then make generic filing *earn* structure over time — that's the funnel, and it's the honest answer to "will it handle my weird documents?": yes, immediately, and it gets smarter.

**Separator sheet — flip the logic, and strip the page.** The redundancy instinct is right; the and-wiring is wrong. A QR code already carries heavy error correction — a misdecode that survives it is practically impossible — so requiring the OCR'd number to *also* agree adds almost no truth but doubles the failure rate: digit OCR on a 200-DPI greyscale skewed scan fails far more often than the QR, and every OCR miss would veto a good split. The right roles: **QR is the decision** (with a namespaced payload like `SFSEP-0007`, so a payment QR printed inside someone's invoice can never trigger a split — namespacing kills the real false-positive risk better than the number check does). **The printed number is the fallback and the human handle** ("put separator 3 before the warranties") — if the QR is creased, number-OCR plus a cheap "does this page look like one of ours?" signature can rescue the split, flagged for review. Because we print the sheet ourselves, design it for the worst scanner: one big QR plus small repeats in the corners, the same mark on both sides (the classic failure is the sheet going through face-down), and instructions printed on the sheet itself. Two non-negotiables: on a confident split the separator page is **removed from the filed PDF** (a separator left inside `Invoice.INV-001.pdf` forever makes the whole feature feel cheap; the untouched original stays in Processed as today), and the failure ladder always lands soft — nothing decodes cleanly means *no* split, with the split point pre-suggested in the existing manual split tool for a one-tap fix. One merged document beats a silent wrong split. Reuse across batches is a feature, not a violation — the numbers are labels, not tokens. And this is the safe way to finally switch separation on for the watch folder: a user-planted, namespaced mark is low-false-positive by construction, where template inference was too risky. Feasibility is clean — an Apache-licensed decoder passes the licence gate, and Tesseract already reads digits.

## The bigger prize

**From filing to finding**
- **My Stuff** — L3, flagship, all home segments (and quietly brilliant for landlords, one per property). File by *thing*, not company: a light "what is this about?" tag (Car, Boiler, House, each child) plus pinned smart collections — saved searches that behave like folders without being folders. Households don't remember issuers; they remember objects. Click "Boiler" and everything's there: receipt, warranty, manual, service history. It's a tag layer over the search that already exists, and it's the demo moment that sells the Home edition.
- **Auto-Title + a {title} filing token** — L2, quick win, everyone. Pick the document's own biggest heading as its title ("Boiler Service Certificate"), editable in one click, usable in filenames. Six months later people remember what a thing was *called*, not its reference number — and folders become browsable by a human.

**The app that earns structure**
- **The Teach-Me Funnel** — L3, flagship, bookkeepers and small offices especially. The system already fingerprints letterheads, so cluster generic confirms by sender and after the fourth one offer: *"You've filed 4 General documents from Aviva — teach this as its own type?"* One click opens the existing Teach Wizard pre-filled, then offers to re-file the back catalogue. Day one needs zero setup; structure arrives when it's earned. Suggestion-only, always through review. (Its field-level twin: a one-off `key: value` note that appears on three docs gets offered promotion to a real field.)
- **Looks Familiar** — L2, medium-term, small business trust feature. A cheap near-duplicate check at confirm: "You filed something very similar on 12 May — view, keep both, or file as a new version." Generic docs are where duplicates breed; version-linking quietly builds a paper trail for contracts and policies.
- **Destination Filing Slips** — L3, medium-term, families, landlords, tradespeople. The slip's code carries a destination or type — "Tax 2026", "Emma — Medical", "everything after this is a Warranty" — so the pile splits *and* pre-sorts, always as a suggestion through review. Sort the paper physically once; the software honours the sort. Print a numbered 10-slip pack from inside the app; a laminated reusable pad is credible merch later.

**The keeper features**
- **Renewal Radar** — L3/L4, the Home headline and the retention engine. Home documents are mostly future obligations: MOT due, insurance renews, warranty ends, passport expires. Mark a date field's role (expires/renews) and show a calm dashboard card: "Car insurance renews in 3 weeks." Offline, derived only from confirmed fields, fails toward silence. This is what makes the app worth opening *between* scans — and the strongest single reason a household pays and stays. Landlords and admins get certificate-lapse protection from the same plumbing.
- **Household Handover Pack** — L4, experimental bet. One-click export of a chosen bundle (wills, policies, deeds) to a password-protected file — "the folder your family needs if something happens to you." Emotionally powerful, technically filtered export plus crypto the product already has.

## Risks & cautions

- **Code signing before any Home launch.** A business buyer clicks past SmartScreen; a family buyer refunds.
- **Consumer support is the real cost of Home** — scanner variance and "where's the Scan button?" Mitigate with a "point your scanner at this folder" first-run step, watch folder on by default, and pricing that assumes support.
- **The watch folder doesn't run the separation pre-pass yet** — close that gap or the Filing Slips demo breaks on the default home path.
- **Don't let Home become a second product.** Everything above is presets, labels, tags, one date rule and one dashboard card over the existing engine. Keep it that way.
- **Market honesty:** paid consumer document software is niche. Treat Home as a funnel and word-of-mouth engine into the £299 tier, not the P&L headline.

## Where I'd start

**First 90 days:** (1) generic type with softened requirements, the one-glance confirm and Auto-Title — smallest work, unblocks everything; (2) Filing Slips, split-only — QR sheet, fail-safe ladder, auto-removed page, printable pack — plus the watch-folder gap; (3) Home Mode, preset pack and the £79 licence.

**Then:** Teach-Me Funnel, My Stuff, destination slips, and Renewal Radar as the Home edition's headline feature. Looks Familiar and the Handover Pack when the base has settled.

**The commercial headline is Filing Slips.** "Print this page, slot it into the pile, press scan" is the demo video, the thing nobody else does trustworthily offline, and the feature that makes the scanner on the desk feel like it was built for ScanFinder. The generic type is the lever, Renewal Radar is the keeper — but the slips are what people will show their friends.
