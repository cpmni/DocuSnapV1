This guide is written for everyday users. You don't need to know anything technical — if you can scan a document and click a button, you can use DocuSnap.

Chapter 1

What DocuSnap does

DocuSnap turns piles of scanned paperwork into a tidy, searchable filing cabinet — automatically.

You point DocuSnap at a folder full of scanned documents (think invoices, orders, and worksheets). It looks at each page, picks out the details that matter — who it's from, the date, the reference number, the total — gives the file a sensible name, and tucks it into a neat folder structure so you can find it again in seconds.

Think of it as a helpful assistant who opens every envelope, reads the document, jots down the key facts, names the file properly, and files it away — without ever getting bored or making typing mistakes.

The best part: it's private

DocuSnap does all of this on your own computer. Your documents are never uploaded to the internet or shared with anyone. Nothing leaves your PC.

Who it's for

Anyone who deals with a steady flow of scanned business paperwork — small business owners, bookkeepers, office administrators, accounts teams — and would rather not rename and file every document by hand.

Chapter 2

Getting started

From opening the app to your first set of filed documents.

Opening the app

Double-click the DocuSnap icon. A small welcome screen with the logo appears for a moment while the app gets ready, then the sign-in screen opens.

Signing in

DocuSnap asks you to sign in so your work is kept under your name. The very first time the app is used, it walks you through creating the first account. After that, you just type your username and password.

There are three kinds of account, each with a different level of access:

Account typeWhat they can do
AdminEverything — process documents, review, search, and change all settings.
EditorProcess documents, review them, and search. Cannot change app-wide settings.
Read OnlySearch and view documents that are already filed. A "look but don't touch" account.

You can change your own password at any time from the account menu in the top-right corner.

The home screen

When you sign in, you land on the home screen. If you haven't processed anything yet, you'll see three big buttons — a launchpad to get you going.

Tell DocuSnap where to file things

Before your first run, pick a destination folder — the place where all your tidy, filed documents will live. You set this once in Settings (see Chapter 7). A folder like Documents\DocuSnap Files works perfectly.

Fast or Smart?

DocuSnap has two working speeds. You choose in Settings:

  • Fast — Quick and light. Great for layouts DocuSnap already knows well.
  • Smart — A little slower, but tries harder when it isn't sure about a document. This is the recommended default.
Tip: Start with Smart. If DocuSnap suggests switching to Fast for a supplier you scan often, that's a good sign it has learned that layout well — see Chapter 5.
Chapter 3

How scanning and filing works

Drop your scans in a folder, press one button, and let DocuSnap do the rest.

Step by step

  1. Scan your documents into a folder on your computer. PDFs and common image files (JPG, PNG, TIFF, BMP) all work. One document per file is ideal.
  2. Open DocuSnap and click Begin Import (or the source-folder box in the sidebar), then choose that folder.
  3. Click Process Documents. DocuSnap works through the folder one file at a time.
  4. Watch the progress. A running list shows each file as it's handled, with a tick for the ones that went smoothly and a flag for any that need your eye.
  5. Review anything flagged (Chapter 4), and you're done — everything is filed.

What "reading" a document means

When DocuSnap "reads" a page, it looks at the picture of the scan and works out the words and numbers on it — just like you would, glancing at a page and seeing the company name and the total. From that, it pulls out the details it needs.

What it pulls out

For each document, DocuSnap looks for the handful of details that matter most:

  • Supplier — who the document is from.
  • Document type — for example an Invoice, a Sales Order, or a Purchase Order.
  • Date — the date on the document.
  • Reference number — the invoice or order number.
  • Total — the amount, where there is one.

How your documents are filed

DocuSnap files everything into a clean, predictable structure inside your destination folder: by company, then year, then month. Each file is renamed so you can tell what it is at a glance, without opening it.

Duplicates are handled for you. If a document looks like one that's already filed, DocuSnap adds a "-DUPLICATE" marker to the new file's name instead of overwriting the original. Nothing is ever lost.
Chapter 4

The Review Queue — checking your documents

Most documents sail straight through. A few need a quick human glance — that's what the Review Queue is for.

Why some documents wait for you

If DocuSnap is fully confident about a document, it files it automatically. If something was unclear — a smudged date, a number it wasn't sure about, or a layout it hasn't seen before — it sets the document aside in the Review Queue and asks you to take a look. The little badge on the Review Queue button tells you how many are waiting.

The review screen

Open the Review Queue and click a document. You'll see the scan on one side and its details on the other. Your job is simply to glance across, fix anything that looks wrong, and confirm.

Your three choices

  • Confirm & File  Everything looks right — file it away. This button only lights up once the essentials (type, date, reference) are filled in.
  • Defer  Not ready to deal with this one yet — set it aside. It moves to the Deferred tab, and you can bring it back with Review Now whenever you like.
  • Delete  Not needed — remove it (you'll be asked to confirm first).
Good to know: When you fix a value and confirm, you're not just correcting one document — you're teaching DocuSnap, so it's more likely to get that supplier right by itself next time.
Chapter 5

How DocuSnap learns and gets better

The more you use it, the smarter it gets. Every correction you make is a small lesson.

The first time DocuSnap sees a new supplier's paperwork, it may need a little help — you might fix a value or two in the Review Queue. Once you confirm that document, DocuSnap remembers the layout: where that supplier prints the date, the reference, the total, and so on. The next document from the same supplier comes through faster and more accurately.

Learned layouts

A learned layout (you may also see these called "templates") is simply DocuSnap's memory of what a particular supplier's document looks like and where the important details sit on the page. You don't have to create these by hand — they build up naturally as you confirm documents. Admins can view and tidy them in Settings.

Grouping similar layouts

Sometimes the same supplier's documents come through in slightly different shapes — a tidy scan one day, a faint one the next. An Admin can put related layouts into a group. When layouts are grouped, they share what's been learned: if DocuSnap has learned exactly where to look on one of them, the others in the group can use that knowledge too. This keeps everything reading correctly even when scans vary.

The "Switch to Fast Mode?" suggestion

After you've confirmed several documents from the same supplier, DocuSnap may pop up a friendly suggestion to switch to Fast mode for that supplier. That's its way of saying, "I know this layout well now — I can handle these quickly." You can accept or dismiss it; nothing breaks either way.

Chapter 6

Finding documents later

Once a document is filed, finding it again takes seconds.

Open Search from the home screen or the top bar. Type what you remember and DocuSnap narrows the list as you go. You can search by:

  • Company — the supplier's name.
  • Reference — the invoice or order number.
  • Date range — from one date to another.
  • Type — Invoice, Sales Order, Purchase Order, and so on.

Pick a result to see a preview and its details. From there you can open the file, open the folder it lives in, or — if it's one you haven't finished checking yet — jump straight back into reviewing it.

Tip: You don't need to fill in every box. Even just a company name or a date range is usually enough to find what you're after.
Chapter 7

Settings, explained simply

Settings is where Admins set things up once so everything else just works. Here's each part in plain words.

General

  • Destination folder — where your filed documents are saved. Click Browse to choose it.
  • Working speed — Fast or Smart (see Chapter 2).
  • How sure it needs to be — a slider that decides how confident DocuSnap must be before filing a document without asking you. Higher means more documents come to the Review Queue for a check; lower means more are filed automatically.

Document Types

The kinds of documents you handle — Invoice, Sales Order, Purchase Order, and any others you add. For each type you can say which detail is its "reference number" and which is its "date," so files are named correctly.

Fields

The individual details DocuSnap looks for on a document (supplier, date, reference, total, and so on). You can add your own for a document type if you need to capture something extra. Built-in details are locked so they can't be removed by accident.

Templates (learned layouts)

Where the layouts DocuSnap has learned are listed. Most people never need to touch this — it fills in by itself as you confirm documents. Admins can rename, group, or tidy layouts here if they wish (see Chapter 5).

Accounts

Add people, set whether each is an Admin, Editor, or Read Only, and manage passwords.

Activation

DocuSnap may run as a free trial for a period, or as a fully activated copy. This screen shows your current status. If you have an activation key, you enter it here to unlock the full version. If you're on a trial, it shows how much time is left.

If DocuSnap asks you to activate: it simply means the trial has ended or the licence needs refreshing. Enter your key on the activation screen, or contact whoever supplied DocuSnap to you. Your documents and settings are never affected.
Chapter 8

Common problems & troubleshooting

Quick answers to the things people ask about most.

If this happens…Try this
A lot of documents land in the Review Queue That's normal at first, especially with new suppliers — DocuSnap is still learning. It eases off as you confirm more. You can also lower "how sure it needs to be" in Settings so more documents file automatically.
The supplier name or a number came out wrong Fix it right in the Review screen and confirm. DocuSnap learns from your correction, so the same supplier should come out right next time.
A document from a familiar supplier suddenly isn't read well Usually a poorer-quality scan. Re-scan it a bit clearer if you can. If you have several layouts for that supplier, an Admin can group them so they share what's learned (Chapter 5).
Nothing happens when I click Process Make sure you've chosen a source folder first (the sidebar box), and that the folder actually contains scans in a supported format (PDF, JPG, PNG, TIFF, BMP).
I can't find a document in Search Try fewer details — just the company or a date range. Remember that documents still waiting in the Review Queue may not be fully filed yet.
I can't open Settings or the Review Queue Those areas depend on your account type. Settings is Admin-only; Review is for Admins and Editors. Ask an Admin if you need access.
DocuSnap is asking me to activate Your trial has ended or the licence needs refreshing. Enter your activation key in Settings → Activation, or contact your supplier. Nothing you've filed is lost.
A warning appears on a screen Read the short message — it usually tells you exactly what to do (for example, "choose a folder" or "this value looks unusual, please check"). When in doubt, confirming the correct value always helps.
Golden rule: when something looks off, fix it and confirm. Almost every problem gets smaller the more you correct, because DocuSnap is always learning from you.
